# Story: Luna's Approval

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Cora the Crow, Rascal the Raccoon, Clover the Deer, Mossy the Turtle
**Reading time:** ~9 minutes

---

Once upon a time, at the edge of the castle meadow, there stood a great old oak.

It was the best tree in the kingdom, and everyone knew why. Its branches spread out wide and low. Its shade was cool in summer and warm in spring. And every single afternoon of her whole life, Princess Savannah's dragon had curled up beneath it. Luna slept there like a black velvet mountain.

That was the oak. That was Luna's spot. Nothing in the kingdom was more settled than that.

But one night, the wind came.

It came off the hills in great rolling pushes, all night long. It rattled the shutters and bent the meadow grass flat. And in the morning, when the sky went quiet and blue again, the great old oak was lying on its side. Roots up, peaceful as a sleeping giant.

The tree was fine. Trees do that sometimes. But Luna was not fine.

Luna had not napped.

And a dragon who has not napped is a dragon with opinions.

Now, on that very morning, Prince Spencer came up the meadow path with a basket on his arm.

Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one.

Actually, there were two who did not know. Savannah was the first. Luna was the second, although with Luna it was hard to tell. Luna might have known everything. Luna might simply have been pretending, which dragons are excellent at.

Spencer had decided something on the walk over. He had decided that before anything else in the world, before a single other thing, he had to win Luna's approval.

He had picked a very bad day for it.

"Good morning," he called, and lifted a hand.

Luna lifted her enormous black head, looked directly at him, and barked.

It was a magnificent bark. It rolled across the meadow and bounced off the castle wall. It went out over the trees, then came back a little later to see how things were going. Three fish in the pond turned around. A baker in the village lost his place in a recipe.

"That's a good bark," said Spencer.

Luna barked again, to make sure he had heard the first one.

On the fence post, a crow fluffed herself up like a small storm cloud.

"THE DRAGON HAS SPOKEN," announced Cora the Crow, to the meadow, the pond, and anyone in the next county. "The prince approaches. The dragon objects. This crow will remain on the scene."

Savannah came out with her water bottle in her hand and her hair not quite brushed. "She's been like this since dawn," she said. "She won't lie down. She won't take a cushion. She won't even eat properly."

"Is she all right?"

"She's Luna," said Savannah, which explained a great deal.

Spencer set his basket in the grass. "I brought her something."

He had, in fact, brought her a great many somethings. He was a wonderful cook, and he had been up since five. The basket held little warm biscuits shaped like clouds.

Luna crossed the meadow in four steps. She took the biscuits. She took the cloth. She took the basket.

She carried all of it twenty steps away and lay down with the basket between her front feet. Then she stared at Spencer while she ate, in case he had thoughts.

"She likes them," said Spencer.

"THE SNACKS HAVE BEEN SEIZED," said Cora. "The basket also. Total loss. This crow is going in closer."

So Spencer tried patience instead.

He sat down in the grass a polite ten steps from the dragon, folded his hands, and waited. Waiting he could do. Waiting he was good at. He had a lot of practice.

Luna finished the basket. Luna considered the prince. Luna stood up, walked over, turned around twice, and sat down directly on top of him.

"Oh," said Spencer, from somewhere underneath.

"PRINCE FLATTENED," reported Cora, hopping along the fence. "Details as they come. He is still visible. Barely."

Savannah laughed so hard she had to sit down in the grass herself. "Do you want help?"

"No," said Spencer, muffled and entirely sincere. "No, this is fine."

But after a while Luna got up, shook herself, and went back to pacing. Back and forth. Back and forth. Grumbling low in her chest like distant thunder that could not find anywhere to rain.

And Savannah stopped laughing and started watching.

She watched Luna push away a cushion with her nose. She watched her try the shade by the wall and get up again. She watched her try the grass by the pond and get up again. She watched her walk all the way over to the fallen oak. She watched her stare at where the shade used to be, and grumble.

And Savannah understood.

"She's not guarding me," she said. "She's not even really mad at you. She's tired, Spencer. She's so tired, and she has nowhere to be tired."

She stood up and brushed off her knees.

"She needs a new spot," said Savannah. "A big one. Today."

Now, when Princess Savannah decides a thing, the meadow tends to rearrange itself around her.

Rascal the Raccoon was sent for soft things. Clover the Deer was sent to find the sunniest patch in the whole meadow. Mossy the Turtle was sent to cross the ground and test it for lumps. He did it at exactly the speed you would expect.

And Spencer was sent to carry the biggest branches of the fallen oak, because nobody else in the kingdom could.

So that is how the nest got built.

It went up the way everything went up with those two. Savannah paced out the shape with her hands in the air. She could see it before it was there. Spencer hauled the logs.

"There," said Savannah. "A little left."

Spencer moved it left.

"No," said Savannah. "My left."

Spencer moved it back, and then a little further. It was just right, and they both knew better than to say so out loud.

Clover came back first, stepping soft, with moss in her mouth. She lined the whole inside of the ring until it looked like green silk.

Rascal came back with three blankets, one cushion, and a scarf.

"Given freely," said Rascal.

"By whom?" said Savannah.

"Freely," said Rascal.

Mossy walked the finished floor from one side to the other, taking his time, feeling for lumps.

"Acceptable," Mossy said at last. "I found two stones. I have moved them. I will move them again if anyone disagrees."

Halfway through, Savannah set her water bottle down in the grass and forgot about it. Spencer picked it up, walked to the meadow spring, and filled it exactly to the line. Not above. Not below. Right to the line, the way she liked it. He set it back in the shade where she would find it.

Savannah found it, drank, and did not say anything. But she smiled at the bottle, which is its own kind of saying something.

And all this time, Luna watched from a distance with her back turned, very obviously not interested.

By late afternoon it was done.

It was a nest fit for a dragon. A wide ring of oak logs. A floor of moss, three blankets, one cushion, and one very suspicious scarf. It sat in the sunniest patch of the whole meadow. It had no lumps. It was, everyone agreed, perfect.

"Luna," called Savannah. "Come see."

Luna came.

Luna sniffed the nest. Luna walked all the way around the nest. Luna barked at the nest, in case it was a trick.

Then Luna lay down in the mud, six steps away, and looked at everybody.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Rascal took off his little hat, which he did not own, and put it back on.

"THE DRAGON HAS REJECTED THE NEST," said Cora, very quietly, which for her was a shout. "This crow has no further information."

Savannah looked at the nest, and then at Luna, and then at the nest again. And she saw it. Nobody had tried it. Luna never took a bed nobody had tried.

"Spencer," she said. "Get in."

So Spencer climbed into the nest. He had carried oak logs all afternoon, and he could feel every single one of them.

He flopped straight back onto the moss with his arms out. He looked up at the sky going gold at the edges.

"Well," he said. "I think it's wonderful."

There was a long pause.

Then there was a sound in the grass. A heavy, unhurried, four-footed sound.

Luna stood up. Luna walked over. Luna stepped into the nest, turned around twice, and lay down directly on top of the prince.

All of her.

"Ohhh," said Spencer.

Savannah crouched down at the edge of the nest, by his head. It was the one place where a small amount of prince was still available.

"She's sitting on you," she said. "That means she loves you."

"I know," Spencer wheezed. "I love her too."

And Luna, warm and enormous and finally, finally still, went to sleep.

She slept the rest of the afternoon. She slept through the shadows getting long. She slept through Rascal quietly taking the scarf back.

Spencer stayed under her the whole time, because moving would have been rude.

Savannah brought him a lemonade with a very long straw, and she held it for him. Then she sat down on the grass beside the nest. She put her arm over the logs and stayed too.

Cora flew to the highest fence post in the kingdom.

"THE DRAGON HAS GIVEN HER APPROVAL," she announced. "The prince has been sat upon. He says he is fine. This crow has seen his face, and he is more than fine."

She said it to the pond. She said it to the orchard. She said it to every tree in the forest before sundown, adding a few details each time. By the last telling, Spencer had carried the whole oak on one shoulder. The nest had a chandelier.

At dusk, Luna woke.

She yawned an enormous yawn with a small flame at the end of it. It looked like a candle being blown the wrong way. She stretched. And then she moved over, exactly enough, and left a Savannah-shaped space on the other side.

Savannah climbed in.

So there they were, the three of them, in one nest in the sunniest patch of the meadow. They watched the first stars come out over the fallen oak.

And Spencer thought, lying there, that this might be the moment. That the words might finally come out in the right order. He took a breath.

Luna snored directly into his ear.

Not tonight, then.

But close.

And they all lived happily ever after.

# Outline: The Prince Who Asked the Eldest Tree

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 10 of 10
**Premise:** Savannah owes the Eldest Tree one new story every year, and this year's is due, and she does not have one. Spencer comes along on the voyage to the Green Island "for the journey," secretly planning to ask a thousand years of wisdom how to say three words. The tree tells him she already knows, and to go home. Savannah's story for the tree turns out to be the story of a prince who kept showing up. They sail home to a bench, a latte, a whole kingdom of conspirators, and a dragon who sits on both of them.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, the Eldest Tree, Big Al, Wilma, and every critter from the series (a crowd at the end)
**Setting:** The sea road to the Green Island, silver rain, the vast rainforest and the Eldest Tree at its heart. Then home: the bench by the pond, at dusk, with the whole kingdom hiding badly in the bushes.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one. Savannah has a problem of her own: her yearly story for the Eldest Tree is due at the next full moon, and she has told the tree every story she knows. Big Al readies the boat. Spencer asks to come, "for the journey," and Al and Wilma exchange the look they have been exchanging for nine stories.

2. **The problem.** All the way across the water, Savannah tries stories on Luna and none of them are new. Spencer, at the tiller, has his own worry: after the bench and the letter and the song and the fog, he has run out of things to make. He has only the three words, and he cannot get them out. He decides to ask the oldest, wisest thing in the world.

3. **The call.** The Green Island, and the silver rain, and the Eldest Tree, who laughs like the world's largest rocking chair to see Savannah again. Savannah asks for one day to find her story. The tree grants it. While Savannah walks the forest with Luna, Spencer slips back alone and stands very small at the roots and asks: "How do I tell her?"

4. **The adventure.** The Eldest Tree, who has listened to every story in the forest for a thousand years, listens to his. The bench. The latte. The nest. The makeover. The letter that wrote back. The song she hummed. The map. The fog. When he is done, the tree is quiet for a long, leafy while. Then she says: "Little prince. She already knows. She has known since the sandpaper. Go home and say it." Spencer says that is not very much advice for a thousand years. The tree laughs so hard it rains.

5. **The twist.** Savannah comes back at moonrise with her story, and sits at the roots, and begins: "Once upon a time, there was a prince who kept showing up." She tells the tree all ten stories, from her side. The bridge and the frog. The cup that tasted like he made it. The dragon who sat on him. The three words she found in Luna's collar and keeps on her windowsill. Spencer, listening in the ferns, realizes she has known everything, the whole time, and has been waiting, kindly, for him. The tree accepts the story as the best she has heard in a hundred years.

6. **The resolution.** They sail home. Word has gone ahead (Cora). At the pond, at dusk, the bench has been polished, a latte waits on it, still cold, and every critter in the kingdom is hiding in the bushes, badly. Big Al and Wilma are not hiding at all. Spencer sits down. Savannah sits down. He takes out the page that says *Just say it.* And he does. Three words, out loud, spelled right. She says, "I know. Me too." Luna climbs onto the bench and lies down on both of them, and the bushes cheer.

7. **Happily ever after.** The fireflies come out. Wilma says, "Finally." Big Al says, "He got there." Somebody starts the song, and everyone knows the rhythm. The prince refills the princess's water bottle for the hundred-and-first time that day, exactly to the line, and the whole kingdom lives happily ever after, and the story goes on, because he keeps showing up.

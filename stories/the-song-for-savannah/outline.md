# Outline: The Song for Savannah

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 7 of 10
**Premise:** Spencer can play drums and bass and a little piano, but he cannot sing a note, so he writes Savannah a song and asks the critters to carry the words, one line each. On the morning of the Music Faire, the Great Drum that opens the Faire has lost its boom, and nobody can find the rhythm to begin. Savannah finds it in the most ordinary place, and Spencer's song becomes the opening number, and she hums along the whole way through without knowing it is about her.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Cora the Crow, Clover the Deer, Thistle the Rabbit, Hazel the Owl, the Fireflies
**Setting:** The Enchanted Music Faire, where instruments play themselves if you ask nicely and the Great Drum in the center square has opened every Faire for a hundred years. Spencer's practice barn, full of drums.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Music Faire eve. Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one. He has written her a song. The rhythm is perfect. The bass line is perfect. The words are good. But when he opens his mouth to sing them, what comes out makes Luna leave the barn. So he does what he always does: he asks for help. Cora takes the loud line. Clover takes the soft one. Thistle takes the fast part. Hazel takes the big question in the middle. They rehearse. It is a mess. It is a wonderful mess.

2. **The problem.** Faire morning. The Great Drum, when struck, goes *thud* instead of *BOOM*. Its skin has gone slack in the damp, and without the Great Drum's boom to set the rhythm, no instrument at the Faire will start. The fiddles wait. The horns wait. A hundred years of tradition, waiting on one drum.

3. **The call.** Savannah says a Faire cannot wait on one drum, and a rhythm has to come from somewhere. She sets out to find one. Spencer says he knows drums and comes along. Luna comes because she has been told there will be sausages.

4. **The adventure.** They try everything. The mill wheel (too slow). The blacksmith's hammer (too loud, and he has to stop for lunch). Woodpeckers (they will not agree on a tempo). Thistle's foot (much too fast). Spencer tightens the Great Drum's ropes with all his strength, and it improves from *thud* to *thump*, which is not enough. Savannah sits down on the edge of the square to think, and puts her hand on Luna's side, and Luna, full of sausage, has fallen asleep.

5. **The twist.** Luna snores. In rhythm. A deep, slow, enormous, perfectly even *hhhrrrmmm... hhhrrrmmm...* And Savannah says, "There it is." She has Spencer roll the Great Drum right up beside the sleeping dragon, and every time Luna breathes out, the drum's slack skin hums with it, a soft boom. Spencer lays his bass line over it. The fiddles catch it. The horns catch it. The Faire begins on a dragon's snore.

6. **The resolution.** The opening number is Spencer's song, because it is the only one already written to that rhythm. Cora belts her line. Clover whispers hers. Thistle rushes his and nobody minds. Hazel asks her big question and the whole square goes quiet. And Savannah, sitting on the step beside the sleeping dragon, hums along the entire way, because the rhythm is one she already knows: it is the tap-tap-tap Spencer's fingers make on the counter every time he refills her water bottle. She never wonders why she knows it. She just hums.

7. **Happily ever after.** The Faire runs until the fireflies come out. Luna wakes for the last song and is told she was the star. She accepts this as obvious. Spencer packs up his drums and Savannah says, "That first song was my favorite. Who wrote it?" And Spencer says, "A friend," which is true, and refills her water bottle.

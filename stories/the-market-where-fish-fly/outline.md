# Outline: The Market Where Fish Fly

**Premise:** Savannah and Luna visit a bustling harbor market where fishmongers toss silver fish through the air like shooting stars and a beloved brass contraption called the Wish-Whirl grants one tiny wish per marble -- until Luna's enthusiastic tail sends all twelve wish-marbles bouncing loose across the market, granting stray little wishes everywhere they land, and the friends must follow the trail of small wonders to bring every marble home before the noon bell. (Part of the Grand Journey set.)
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Luna, Rascal the Raccoon, Pip the Jumping Spider, Otto the Otter (new -- the old tinker who keeps the Wish-Whirl)
**Setting:** The Harbor Market -- a bright, loud, wonderful crush of stalls along the waterfront: fishmongers who sing and throw their catch to each other over the crowd, flower stalls exploding with color, a cheese man with wheels taller than Savannah, gulls with opinions, and in the middle of it all, the Wish-Whirl: a tall brass machine full of pins and chutes and bells, polished for fifty years by Otto the Otter.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Market morning. Savannah and Luna arrive by the harbor road and take it all in -- the singing fishmongers tossing silver fish in high arcs ("LOW BRIDGE!"), the flower stalls, the smells, the noise. And the Wish-Whirl: drop a copper button in the top, and a glass marble goes rattling and bouncing down through a forest of brass pins, ringing tiny bells, until it lands in one of the little cups at the bottom -- and grants one tiny wish. Nothing grand. Warm-socks-sized wishes. Otto the Otter, the old tinker who keeps it, explains the one rule everyone knows: the marbles must all be home in the machine when the noon bell rings, because at noon the Wish-Whirl sings, and it can't sing missing a single marble.

2. **The problem.** A fishmonger tosses a particularly magnificent salmon in a high silver arc, and Luna -- overcome with enthusiasm -- spins to track it. Her tail catches the Wish-Whirl. The top pops open, and all twelve wish-marbles leap free and go bouncing off into the market in twelve different directions. And wherever a wish-marble lands, a stray tiny wish comes true: a fiddle starts playing itself, a fountain runs lemonade, a gull begins speaking only in rhyme, a stack of cabbages starts juggling itself.

3. **The call.** Otto isn't angry -- but he checks his pocket watch: one hour to the noon bell. Savannah promises to bring every marble home in time. Rascal the Raccoon (nobody in any market is better at finding things) and Pip the Jumping Spider (nobody anywhere sees small details better) sign on at once. Luna, deeply embarrassed, appoints herself chief of transport: found marbles ride home in a fold of her wing.

4. **The adventure.** They track the marbles by following the small wonders each one caused -- "follow the strange, find the marble." Counting rhythm through the market: the self-playing fiddle (marble under the fiddler's stool), the lemonade fountain (marble in the basin), the rhyming gull (marble in its nest on the awning -- Pip climbs for it), the juggling cabbages (marble in the crate -- Rascal negotiates), and so on, weaving through stalls, over rooftops, along the dock. Eleven marbles ride home in Luna's wing, one by one by one.

5. **The twist (two turns).** First: the twelfth marble is nowhere -- until Pip, who sees what nobody else sees, spots it glinting from between the scales of Luna's own tail. It had bounced straight back into the tail that set it loose, and it never granted its wish at all; it's been saving it, warm and ready. Second: as they hurry back, Savannah realizes the stray wishes were never chaos. The fiddler's fiddle had been playing the tune his grandmother used to play. The lemonade fountain poured on the hottest day of the summer. The rhyming gull had made a lonely dockhand laugh for the first time in a week. Otto confirms the machine's oldest secret: wish-marbles never land wrong.

6. **The resolution.** With the crowd counting along, eleven marbles go home -- and Otto says the twelfth, the one with the unspent wish, is Savannah's to spend, since it chose to ride with her crew. Savannah keeps it tiny, the way the Wish-Whirl likes: she wishes for one more song before noon. The whole market gets it -- fishmongers, fiddler, flower-sellers, the rhyming gull taking harmony -- everyone singing as the last marble rattles down through the pins.

7. **Happily ever after.** The noon bell rings, and the Wish-Whirl sings its noon song with all twelve marbles home -- a sound like a music box the size of a lighthouse. The fishmonger whose salmon started it all tosses Luna a fish of her own ("for the dragon with the eventful tail"), which Luna catches with enormous dignity. Sailing home at golden hour, full of market bread and one perfect small song.

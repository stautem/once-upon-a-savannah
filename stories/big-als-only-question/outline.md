# Outline: Big Al's Only Question

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 1 of 10
**Premise:** Prince Spencer goes to Big Al expecting a grand test to prove himself worthy of Princess Savannah, and gets one question instead: "What makes her laugh?" He spends a rainy week trying everything the critters can coach him through, and fails, until a washed-out footbridge, a hammer, and a frog show him the answer was never a trick at all.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Big Al, Wilma (a look, a line), Finnegan the Fox, Thistle the Rabbit, Ollie the Opossum
**Setting:** A rainy week in the kingdom. Big Al's workshop on the hill, where the fire always burns and the door is always half open. The creek at the bottom of the meadow, with its little wooden footbridge, where the critters cross every evening for the story-circle.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Rain, the soft kind, for six days straight. Everyone cozy indoors. Prince Spencer is introduced the way every story in this series begins: "Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one." He is kind, he is strong, he makes the best lavender lemonade anyone has tasted, and he refills Savannah's water bottle a hundred times a day. He is brave about everything except one thing. So he climbs the hill to Big Al, who has judged dragons and mended kingdoms, and asks for a test.

2. **The problem.** Big Al sets down his hammer, thinks a long time, and asks one question: "What makes her laugh?" Spencer does not know. He knows her favorite drinks, her favorite places, the exact way she likes her water bottle filled (to the line, not over). But he has never made her laugh on purpose. Al says come back when you know.

3. **The call.** Spencer enlists coaches. Finnegan teaches him three jokes (Spencer mixes up the punchlines). Thistle teaches him a very fast dance (Spencer is not fast). Ollie offers a dramatic finale: Spencer will pretend to faint, and Ollie will play dead beside him (Ollie plays dead too well and has to be carried home). Savannah smiles at each attempt, politely, kindly, the way you smile at a friend trying hard. It is not a laugh.

4. **The adventure.** On the seventh evening the creek rises and takes the footbridge with it. The critters on the far bank cannot cross for story-circle. Savannah does not wait for anyone: she gathers rope, boards, and the biggest hammer in the kingdom, and heads down through the rain. Spencer follows, carrying everything. On the bank the two of them work the way they always work: she measures, he lifts; she levels, he holds; she points, he hammers. Luna stands in the creek as a living pillar, complaining the whole time.

5. **The twist.** On the last nail Spencer hits his thumb, says "OW" in a very un-princely voice, hops backward, and sits down in the shallow creek. When he comes up there is a frog on his head. The frog looks at Savannah. Savannah looks at the frog. And she laughs, the real laugh, the one that bends her in half, until she has to sit down on the new bridge to catch her breath. Spencer, dripping, frog and all, starts laughing too.

6. **The resolution.** The bridge holds. The critters cross. Story-circle happens under Luna's wing in the rain, and Savannah keeps glancing at Spencer and laughing all over again. The next morning Spencer climbs the hill and tells Big Al: "She laughs when I'm just me." Big Al nods slowly. "Took me a while to learn that one too." From the doorway Wilma catches Al's eye, and the two elders share a look that says the whole kingdom is about to be in on something.

7. **Happily ever after.** Spencer walks Savannah home, refills her water bottle for the hundredth time that day, exactly to the line. She says, "You still have creek in your hair." He says he knows. Everyone warm, everyone laughing, rain tapping the roof.

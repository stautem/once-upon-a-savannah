# Outline: The Bench That Took All Winter

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 5 of 10
**Premise:** Spencer asks Big Al to teach him to build a bench for Savannah, all by himself, as a gift. Al hands him a board and says "sand it." Winter comes and goes, and Spencer never gets past the sanding. When the old stargazing bench by the frozen pond cracks in the cold, Savannah rolls up her sleeves, and Al reveals the lesson he was waiting all winter to teach: nobody builds a bench alone.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Big Al, Mossy the Turtle, Digby the Mole, the Fireflies
**Setting:** Big Al's woodshop through a whole winter, sawdust and woodsmoke and a stove that ticks. The pond at the bottom of the meadow, frozen and silver, with the old bench where the kingdom sits to watch winter stars.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** First snow. Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one. He has decided to build her something with his own two hands, which is brave, because his own two hands are wonderful at pans and drums and wires, and hopeless at wood. He tells Big Al he wants to build a bench. Alone. Al looks at him for a while, hands him a board and a square of sandpaper, and says, "Sand it."

2. **The problem.** Spencer sands. He sands through December. He sands through the long dark of January. Every time he brings the board to Al, Al runs a thumb along it and says, "Nearly." Spencer tries a second board: the saw wanders. A third: the legs come out three different lengths. Mossy, who sits by the stove all winter, says, "Some things take the time they take." Spencer sands.

3. **The call.** The coldest night of the year cracks the old bench by the pond clean in two. The kingdom's stargazing spot is gone, and the Long Night Stars, the ones that only come once a winter, are due in three days. Savannah takes one look and says, "Then we'll build a new one." Spencer, panicking, says he has one nearly finished. This is not true. He has one very smooth board.

4. **The adventure.** Savannah arrives at the woodshop with her level, her measuring string, and Luna carrying the tools. She sees the very smooth board and the three-legged wreckage, and she does not laugh. She says, "Okay. Show me what you've got." And they build it the way they build everything: she measures and marks, he cuts where she marks; she checks the level, he drills where she points; she holds the leg, he drives the peg. Digby brings up a flat stone for the base from underground. Mossy tests every seat.

5. **The twist.** Spencer, ashamed, admits it was supposed to be a gift he made alone. Big Al, who has said almost nothing all winter, finally sets down his mug. "I know. That's why I gave you sandpaper." He never expected Spencer to build it alone. Nobody builds a bench alone. "Al didn't build this shop alone. Wilma didn't build her kitchen alone. The best things in this kingdom have two sets of hands on them." Spencer's smooth board, sanded all winter, becomes the seat, and it is the smoothest seat anyone has ever sat on.

6. **The resolution.** On the third night they carry the bench down to the pond together, Spencer on one end, Luna on the other, Savannah walking ahead with a lantern. It sits level on Digby's stone. The Long Night Stars come out. The whole kingdom takes turns on the bench, and it holds all of them, even Luna. Spencer thinks: I could say it now. Savannah says, "It's a good bench. It's *our* bench." He decides that is enough for tonight.

7. **Happily ever after.** Fireflies who should be asleep for winter come out anyway for the occasion. Big Al and Wilma sit on the bench last, after everyone has gone in, and Al says, "He's getting there." Wilma says, "He's been there since the sandpaper." The bench is still by the pond. It has never wobbled once.

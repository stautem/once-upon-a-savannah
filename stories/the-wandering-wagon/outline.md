# Outline: The Wandering Wagon

**Premise:** Big Al and Wilma arrive in a magical wagon that rolls only when its lantern is fed with wonder, and they take Savannah and Luna on a grand journey to the far wonders of the world -- a palace built of golden corn, canyons striped like sunset, an inn where stardust drifts down the chimney -- until the lantern dims on a long gray road and Savannah must discover that wonder isn't something you find, it's something you notice. (First of the Grand Journey set.)
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Luna, Big Al, Wilma, Cora the Crow
**Setting:** The open road beyond the kingdom's farthest hill. The Wandering Wagon itself: a snug wooden wagon with a round door, bunks that fold down from the walls, a kettle that never quite stops steaming, and a brass lantern hanging from the front bow. The lantern glows when someone aboard is filled with wonder -- and the wagon only rolls while the lantern glows.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Savannah has explored every corner of her kingdom -- every meadow, every stream, every mossy stone -- and lately she's been climbing the farthest hill just to look at the road on the other side. Luna comes along and barks at the horizon, which does not bark back.

2. **The problem / the call.** One morning the Wandering Wagon comes up the road, with Big Al driving and Wilma reading a map that seems to have more places on it every time she unfolds it. The wagon comes "once in a great while, when someone is ready." They invite Savannah and Luna on the Great Loop -- the journey to the far wonders. Wilma explains the rule: the lantern runs on wonder, and the wagon rolls only while the lantern glows. Cora the Crow appoints herself official journey reporter and perches on the roof.

3. **The adventure.** Three stops, each with the refrain "The lantern glowed. The wagon rolled." (a) The Palace of Golden Corn -- an entire palace built of corn, with murals of harvest suns and dancing crows on its walls; the corn-keepers give Savannah a single golden kernel for the shelf above the lantern. (b) The Painted Canyons -- striped stone hills in ribbons of rose and gold and dusty purple, like sunset that decided to stay; Savannah keeps a striped pebble. (c) The Stardust Inn -- a creaky, cozy inn in the silver mountains where stardust drifts down the chimney at night and settles on the windowsills; the innkeeper sweeps a pinch into a tiny glass bottle for her. Each treasure goes on the shelf above the lantern.

4. **The twist.** The Long Gray Flats: a road with no palaces, no canyons, no mountains -- just flat gray grass to the horizon. The lantern dims. The wagon slows, then stops. Cora announces breaking news: STRANDED. But Wilma isn't worried. She says the lantern doesn't run on grand things -- it runs on wonder, and wonder is a way of looking. Savannah kneels down in the gray grass and looks closely: a beetle with a shell like polished treasure, the smell of rain coming, Big Al whistling music through a blade of grass. Luna discovers her own shadow and has opinions about it. The lantern flickers -- then blazes brighter than it has the whole trip.

5. **The resolution.** The wagon rolls again, faster and lighter than ever, because now everything feeds the lantern -- ordinary birds, ordinary puddles, the ordinary moon. They crest the last hills to the sea, where fish leap like silver birds in a far harbor and a green island full of ancient trees drinks the fog (winks at the rest of the Grand Journey set), and then the road bends for home.

6. **Happily ever after.** Home again. The treasures from the journey sit on Savannah's windowsill -- the golden kernel, the striped pebble, the little bottle of stardust -- and at night they glow softly, each with the light of the place it came from. The journey comes home with you. Luna is asleep before the first star is out, and the lantern on the resting wagon glows gently all night long, because home, looked at closely, is full of wonder too.

# Outline: Pia the Peacekeeper

**Premise:** In a serene garden of stone lanterns, raked silver sand, and koi ponds lives Pia, a grand peacock whose fanned tail settles every quarrel in the garden -- but when Finnegan the Fox and Thistle the Rabbit fall into a squabble so stubborn they won't even look up at her tail, Savannah discovers that keeping the peace sometimes means listening longer than anyone else is willing to. (Part of the Grand Journey set.)
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Luna, Pia the Peacock (new), Finnegan the Fox, Thistle the Rabbit, Cora the Crow (cameo)
**Setting:** The Garden of Stone Lanterns -- a walled garden of quiet wonders: silver sand raked into ripples like a frozen pond, a red arched bridge over a koi pond, stone lanterns that hold a soft glow at dusk, and pines clipped into clouds. The most peaceful place in the kingdom. Usually.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** The Garden of Stone Lanterns, and how peace is kept there: whenever a quarrel starts among the garden's residents, Pia the Peacock walks calmly to the middle of it and fans her tail -- a hundred shimmering blue-green eyes -- and it is simply impossible to stay angry while looking at it. Everyone stops, breathes, remembers to be gentle. It has worked for as long as anyone can remember.

2. **The problem.** Savannah and Luna arrive for a visit and find the garden all wrong: sand kicked out of its ripples, koi hiding under the bridge, and from across the moss hedge, the worst squabble the garden has ever heard -- Finnegan the Fox and Thistle the Rabbit, neighbors and (until this week) best friends, shouting over the hedge. Finnegan says Thistle trampled his moon-viewing moss patch. Thistle says Finnegan stole his lucky striped pebble. Pia has fanned her tail three times -- and for the first time ever, it didn't work. They wouldn't even look up. Pia's tail droops in the dust; she believes she has lost her gift.

3. **The call.** Savannah tells Pia the gift isn't gone -- but maybe this quarrel needs something before the tail can work. She decides to do what nobody has done yet: stop trying to quiet the quarrel, and start listening to it. Luna settles by the koi pond to supervise the fish (the koi find her fascinating; she finds them suspicious).

4. **The adventure.** Savannah listens to each friend separately, longer than is comfortable -- past the accusations, into the hurt underneath. Finnegan (once the jokes run out) admits he'd been saving his best joke all week to tell Thistle, and thought the trampled moss meant Thistle didn't care. Thistle (once he slows down) admits the "lucky pebble" was lucky because Finnegan gave it to him, and losing it felt like losing the friendship. Then detective work: the moss patch shows small pressed footprints and -- glinting at its center -- nothing. Both clues point the same direction: something small crossed the moss to reach something shiny.

5. **The twist.** Cora the Crow, dramatic as ever, arrives mid-investigation with breaking news and freezes: the lucky striped pebble is tucked in her satchel of shiny things. She "borrowed" it at dawn for her collection -- and it was her hopping across the moss patch to reach it that left the tracks. She confesses at full volume and returns it with the deepest, most theatrical apology the garden has ever seen. Mystery solved -- but the quarrel doesn't end, because by now each friend has said prickly things, and the argument has grown bigger than its cause. That's the real knot: not the pebble, not the moss. So Savannah tells each what the other told her -- the saved-up joke, why the pebble was lucky -- and their heads come up, and their ears soften. And *that* is when Savannah nods to Pia.

6. **The resolution.** Pia rises, walks to the hedge, and fans her tail -- a hundred blue-green eyes catching the lantern light -- and this time both friends are looking, and the peace settles over the garden like a blanket being smoothed. Finnegan tells the saved-up joke; Thistle laughs so hard he falls over; the pebble goes back on Thistle's windowsill where both of them can see it over the hedge. Pia tells Savannah the truth she'd forgotten: the tail never made the peace. It only reminds folks of the peace they already want. The listening is the peacekeeping.

7. **Happily ever after.** Evening: the stone lanterns glow, the sand is raked into fresh ripples -- with three new swirls in the corner, one for each friend -- the koi come out from under the bridge (and do a slow circle around the sleeping Luna, whom they have decided is a very large, very quiet koi). Tea on the red bridge. Pia's tail shimmers in the lantern light, not because anyone is quarreling, but just because it's beautiful.

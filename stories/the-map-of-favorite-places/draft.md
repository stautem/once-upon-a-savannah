# Story: The Map of Favorite Places

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Bramble the Bat, Digby the Mole, Clover the Deer, Hazel the Owl
**Reading time:** ~9 minutes

---

Once upon a time, in a kingdom with a great deal of wood and only a little bit of castle, there lived a prince. He was very good at showing up.

His name was Spencer.

Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one.

The critters knew. The fireflies knew. Luna the dragon had known the longest of anybody. She was also the worst at keeping it, because she had never kept anything quiet in her life.

Spencer had almost everything ready.

He had the drink, an iced oat milk lavender latte, and he could make it exactly right every time now.

He had the bench, with a seat he had sanded himself all winter, because Spencer and saws were not friends, but Spencer and sandpaper were.

He had the song, four notes he could play without looking at his hands.

And he had three words, folded up small and carried in his pocket. They had gotten very soft at the edges.

What he did not have was the place.

"It has to be the right place," Spencer told the empty kitchen one morning. "Somewhere she loves. The most somewhere she loves."

So he went to ask the two in the kingdom who knew where everything was.

Bramble the Bat knew every path in the wood by night. She flew the dark the way other folk walked a hallway. Quiet and sure, and never once bumping into anything.

Digby the Mole knew every path under the wood by touch. He was soft and round and velvety, and he spoke so gently that you had to lean in. Leaning in toward Digby was one of the nicer things a person could do with a day.

"A map," said Bramble.

"A map," agreed Digby.

"A star," said Bramble, "for every place the princess loves."

So they made one.

---

The trouble started right away, and the trouble was this: the map would not stay finished.

They inked the lavender hill first, because that was easy. A star.

Then the bench by the pond. A star.

Then the footbridge where the water talks to itself. Wilma's kitchen, which smelled of cardamom no matter the season. The Music Faire square, where Savannah had once danced with a dragon and everyone had gotten out of the way.

Five stars. Six. Nine. The map was beautiful.

And then Savannah found a hollow log that echoed.

She came home delighted about it. She had said "hello" into it and the log had said "hello" back, twice, and a little smaller each time.

Bramble sighed and inked a tenth star.

The next week Savannah found a rock at the edge of the meadow that stayed warm long after the sun went down. Eleven.

Then a puddle that caught the moon so perfectly you could kneel down and have the whole moon to yourself. Twelve.

Bramble flew out one night and came back at dawn with three more. Once she started looking, she could not stop seeing them. Digby added an entire underground level. It was mostly halls made of roots, and one small cave with a dripping sound he described as "companionable."

The map grew a second sheet.

Then, a month later, a third.

Spencer sat on the kitchen floor with all three sheets unrolled and his chin in his hands. He could not choose. Every star was perfect. That was the whole problem. There were too many perfect places, and a person only gets to say three words for the first time once.

Luna came and lay down across the corner of sheet two. That is a thing dragons do when they feel a decision is taking too long.

"I know," said Spencer.

Luna groaned like a door.

"I know," said Spencer.

---

It was the following Tuesday that Clover did not come home.

Clover the Deer was gentle and shy and did not say much. But she always showed up when it mattered, which is its own kind of talking. She had gone out at dawn. It was nearly dusk.

Nobody panicked. Clover was careful, and the wood was kind, and nothing in that kingdom had ever meant anybody any harm.

But the light was going gold, and then going grey, and Clover was still not back.

Savannah stood in the doorway of the castle kitchen and thought.

Then she said, "Clover goes where I go. She always has."

The critters looked up.

"Every place I love, she's been there with me," said Savannah. "Standing off to the side, being quiet, keeping an eye. If we know my favorite places, we know hers."

There was a small sound from the corner of the kitchen.

It was Spencer, clearing his throat, very quietly.

"I might," he said, "have something that could help."

---

He brought out the three sheets and unrolled them across the kitchen table, one, two, three. The last corner would not lie flat, so he weighed it down with a jar of honey.

Savannah leaned over the stars. The lavender hill. The bench. The footbridge. Wilma's kitchen. The square. The echoing log. The warm rock. The moon puddle. All of it, inked and neat, every place she had ever loved out loud.

"Who made this?" she asked.

"Friends," said Spencer.

Savannah looked at him for about one second longer than she needed to.

Then she picked up the first sheet, because there was work to do.

"Star by star," she said. "Let's go."

Luna went first, because Luna always went first, announcing the expedition to the entire wood whether the wood wanted it or not. Bramble took the air. Digby took the under. Hazel the Owl came along too, gliding ahead of everyone and asking large questions of nobody in particular. "What is a home, really?" she said. And, "Do we find places, or do places find us?"

"Hazel," said Savannah kindly.

"I'll ask them quieter," said Hazel.

They went to the echoing log first.

Savannah knelt and called Clover's name, softly, the way Clover liked it. Not a shout. Clover did not care for shouts.

"Clover?"

The log said *Clover* back. Twice. Smaller each time.

Empty.

They went to the warm rock next. Savannah put her hand flat on it, and it was warm, and nobody was there.

"Clover?" she called, soft.

Empty.

They went to the moon puddle, and the whole moon was in it, waiting for somebody to kneel down, and nobody was.

"Clover?"

Empty.

Three stars, three quiet calls, three empty places.

At the lavender hill Spencer took Savannah's water bottle without being asked. He filled it at the spring, exactly to the line, the way he always did. Not over. Not under. Exactly to the line, which is a thing nobody had ever taught him and nobody had ever asked for.

Savannah drank, and handed it back, and did not say thank you, because by now it was past thank you.

They went to the bench by the pond. They went to the footbridge, where the water was talking to itself about the evening. Bramble scouted wide circles overhead. At each stop Digby came up out of the ground like a small velvet periscope, shook his head, and went back down.

Star by star, all the way out to the edge of the third sheet.

And then Bramble came back.

She came back fast, which Bramble never did, and she landed on Savannah's shoulder and said one word.

"Farther."

---

It was past the last star.

It was past the edge of the third sheet, out where the ink stopped and the paper was just paper.

Bramble led them to a gap in the trees that nobody had ever bothered to walk. The trees opened up, and there was a meadow.

Nobody had mapped it. Nobody had known it was there.

And the whole meadow, corner to corner, was lavender.

It went purple all the way to the far trees, moving a little in the evening air. It held the last of the sun the way a cup holds tea. And standing in the middle of it, perfectly still and perfectly happy, was Clover. Ears up, watching the sky go pink and orange and gold.

She was not lost.

She had found a new favorite place before Savannah did, and she had stayed to watch the sunset in it.

Savannah walked in through the lavender and stood beside her, shoulder to shoulder. She looked at what Clover was looking at.

And Savannah said, "Oh."

That was all. One word.

Spencer stood at the edge of the meadow and watched her face. That was when he finally understood the thing he had been getting wrong for three whole sheets of paper.

The map would never be finished. Not ever. Savannah was never going to stop finding new places to love. No ink in the world could get ahead of her.

He could stand next to her and hear her say "Oh" anywhere. He had been doing it all along.

---

They walked home in the dark, and the dark was easy. Bramble knew the paths. Digby knew the shortcuts. Luna made enough noise to keep any worry away.

Clover walked in the middle. That is where you put the one who was missing.

Luna carried the map rolled up under her wing. She held it with enormous seriousness, and looked around several times to be sure everyone had noticed.

At the kitchen table, Spencer took the pen and added one more star. It sat right at the edge of the third sheet, out where the paper had been blank that morning.

Beside it he wrote the name, and the letters came out sideways, the way letters always did for him:

*Clovers Medow.*

Savannah picked up the pen, added the apostrophe, fixed the *e*, and set the pen down.

She did not say one single word about it.

Digby cleared his throat softly and said that there was probably a tunnel that went out that way.

There was one by morning.

---

The map hangs in the castle kitchen to this day, all three sheets, corner to corner. A jar of honey still holds down the end.

Anyone who finds something worth a star may add one. Bramble adds hers at night. Digby adds his from underneath, which makes a small bump in the paper. Luna does not add stars, but she supervises, loudly.

It has never been finished. That is the best thing about it.

And Prince Spencer stopped looking for the right place.

He still had the drink, and the bench, and the song, and three very soft words in his pocket.

But he had decided the right place was simply wherever she was standing when he finally opened his mouth.

And they all lived happily ever after.

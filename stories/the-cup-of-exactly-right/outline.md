# Outline: The Cup of Exactly Right

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 2 of 10
**Premise:** Spencer asks Wilma to teach him Savannah's favorite drink, the iced oat milk lavender latte, and cannot get it right. Every cup is too bitter, too sweet, or tastes like soap, because he keeps rushing. Meanwhile the lavender field on the hill has closed its blossoms tight and will not open, and Savannah sets out to learn why. Both problems have the same answer: slow down and listen.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Wilma, Hazel the Owl, Pepper the Skunk, Digby the Mole
**Setting:** Wilma's kitchen, which smells like every good thing at once and has a copper kettle that sings when the water is exactly right. The lavender field on the hill above the village, purple to the horizon, with a thin silver stream that waters its roots.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Summer. The lavender is in bloom and the whole kingdom smells like it. Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one. He can cook anything, but there is one drink he has never made right, and it is her favorite. So he knocks on Wilma's door with a jar of oat milk, and Wilma, who sees everything, says only: "Ah. Come in."

2. **The problem.** Wilma has one rule: "The cup knows when you're rushing." Spencer's first cup is bitter (he boiled the tea). His second is sweet enough to hum (he poured with his eyes closed). His third tastes like soap (too much lavender, too fast). Wilma sips each, says "Mm," and pours it in the garden. Meanwhile, up on the hill, Savannah discovers that every lavender blossom has closed up tight, the way they do before a storm. But the sky is blue and no storm comes.

3. **The call.** Savannah, with Luna, goes to find out why. Hazel the Owl comes because she has a theory (she has several). Pepper the Skunk comes because lavender is her favorite smell and she is very delicate about it. Down in the kitchen, Wilma tells Spencer the fourth cup can wait: "You can't make her favorite drink from lavender that's sulking. Go help."

4. **The adventure.** On the hill Savannah walks the rows and listens. The blossoms are fine. The soil is dry. The silver stream that waters the field has gone to a trickle. They follow it uphill and find the reason: Digby the Mole's newest tunnel has shifted a stone, and the stream has wandered off into the meadow. Digby is mortified. Savannah says there is nothing to be sorry for, just something to fix. She lays out a line of stones to guide the water back. Spencer carries the big ones. Savannah places them, one, two, three, and sits back to watch. Nothing happens. She waits. Nothing happens. Luna sighs loudly.

5. **The twist.** Hazel says, "Water doesn't hurry for anyone." So they stop hurrying. They sit in the lavender and Pepper tells a story, and the stream, in its own time, finds the new path, and creeps, and runs, and rushes home to the roots. One by one by one, the blossoms open, in a wave rolling down the hill like a purple sigh. Spencer, sitting still for once, notices that the stream makes the same sound as a kettle when it runs right.

6. **The resolution.** Back in the kitchen, Wilma hands him fresh lavender that opened today. Spencer fills the kettle, and this time he does not stir, does not check, does not fuss. He listens for the kettle's song. When it sings, he pours. Ice, oat milk, lavender, slow. Wilma sips. "Exactly right." Savannah comes in from the hill, takes the cup, and says: "This tastes like you made it." Spencer does not know what to say, so he refills her water bottle.

7. **Happily ever after.** As he leaves, Wilma says, quite casually, "I've known since you asked for oat milk." Spencer turns red. Wilma smiles and goes back to her kettle. Up on the hill the lavender sways, and the whole kingdom smells like exactly right.

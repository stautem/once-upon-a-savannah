# Story: The Dragon's Bad Day

**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Thistle the Rabbit, Hazel the Owl, Rascal the Raccoon
**Reading time:** ~8 minutes

---

Once upon a time, in a kingdom with a tower that caught the very first light of morning, there lived a dragon. She greeted every single day the exact same way.

Loud. Loud, and first up, and absolutely certain the whole kingdom needed to know she was awake.

Luna's mornings went like this: a great black-scaled stretch, a rumbling good-morning roar that rattled the teacups, and a determined march to whoever she loved most to make sure they knew it. It was one of the kingdom's small, reliable comforts, as steady as the sunrise itself.

Until one grey, cloudy morning, it wasn't.

Savannah came downstairs expecting the roar and got quiet instead. She found Luna curled in a corner, not stretching, not rumbling, not marching anywhere. Her tail was still. Her great head was down.

"Luna?" said Savannah. "Good morning."

Luna opened one eye, sighed a small sigh, and closed it again.

Savannah brought her favorite treat — she turned her head away. She checked every scale for a scrape or a sore spot — there wasn't one. She wasn't hurt. She wasn't sick. She was just *off*, in a way that was somehow more worrying than anything with an obvious fix.

"Something's wrong," Savannah said, "and I don't know what."

So she gathered help. Thistle the Rabbit came at a hop, ears up, ready to solve it before she'd even heard the problem. Hazel the Owl came blinking and thoughtful. And Rascal the Raccoon came already turning ideas over in his clever hands, Prince Spencer right behind him, mostly there to sit close by Luna and keep her company while the others puzzled it out.

"Maybe she's hungry," said Thistle, and brought three different snacks, one after another, each offered with great hope.

Luna turned her head away from all three.

"Maybe she's hurt somewhere we missed," said Hazel, and they checked again, wingtip to tail, every last scale.

Nothing.

"Maybe she's bored," said Rascal, and fetched her very favorite game, the one that never once in her whole life had failed to get her tail thumping.

Luna looked at it, sighed, and closed her eyes.

Three good guesses. Three good tries. Nothing had worked, and Luna stayed exactly as quiet as she'd been since sunrise.

Spencer didn't say much through any of it. He just sat with her, one hand resting on her big black shoulder, not needing her to explain a thing.

And that was how he noticed it.

Every minute or so, without seeming to realize she was doing it, Luna turned her head toward the window. Toward the flat, grey, sunless sky. Looked at it for a moment. Sighed. Looked away. And then, a minute later, did it again.

"Savannah," he said. "Look."

Savannah watched her do it once more, and then it clicked into place, quiet and certain.

"She's not hungry, and she's not hurt, and she's not bored," Savannah said slowly. "She's missing her sun."

Every morning, before anything else, Luna climbed to the top of the tower and stretched out in the very first light. She soaked it in the way only a black-scaled dragon can. It wasn't a meal. It wasn't a game. It was just *hers*, the one small thing that made a morning feel like a morning — and today, for the first time anyone could remember, there was no sun to be had.

"We can't fix the sky," said Spencer.

"No," said Savannah, already smiling. "But we can fix the rest of it."

They carried blankets up to the tower, warm ones, and piled them into a nest against the grey. Hazel found every candle in the castle and set them glowing in a ring. Thistle and Rascal curled up close on either side of Luna. And Savannah and Spencer settled in right against her, one on each side, until there wasn't a single cold or lonely spot left anywhere near her.

It wasn't sunlight. But it was warm, and it was close, and it was exactly the shape of the thing she'd been missing.

Luna's tail gave one small thump against the tower floor.

Then another.

Then she lifted her great head, looked around at everyone piled in close, and let out a roar so loud and so pleased that every teacup in the castle rattled at once.

"There she is," said Savannah, laughing.

Luna nuzzled Spencer first, hard enough to nearly knock him over, for noticing what nobody else had. Then she nuzzled everyone else in turn, one by one, until the whole tower was full of noise and warmth and one very large, very happy dragon.

By the time the clouds finally broke that afternoon and real sunlight came pouring through, Luna barely noticed. She already had everything she'd been missing, piled in close around her on the tower floor.

*...and they all lived happily ever after.*

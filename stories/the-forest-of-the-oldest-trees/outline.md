# Outline: The Forest of the Oldest Trees

**Premise:** A ferry carries Savannah and Luna across the water to a hushed green island where moss hangs like curtains and the trees are the oldest, wisest beings in the kingdom -- but the Eldest Tree has drifted into such a deep sleep that the forest's gentle rain has stopped, and every loud way of waking her fails. The answer is the quietest kind of bravery: the Eldest has listened to every story in the forest for a thousand years, and no one has ever told *her* one. (Part of the Grand Journey set.)
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Luna, Mossy the Turtle, Digby the Mole, Clover the Deer
**Setting:** The Green Island, out in the fog across the water -- an ancient rainforest where moss drapes from every branch like soft green curtains, fallen giants nurse whole rows of young trees on their backs, and the air is so quiet it feels like being inside a held breath. At its heart stands the Eldest Tree, so wide that ten Savannahs holding hands couldn't circle her trunk.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** A slow knock at the castle door: Mossy the Turtle, who has been traveling for three days to deliver a message that fits in one sentence. The Green Island's rain has stopped. On an island where soft silver rain falls almost every day and everything green depends on it, the moss is going crisp at the edges. Mossy's cousins on the island sent word the fast way (a gull), then the reliable way (Mossy).

2. **The call.** Savannah has heard of the Green Island all her life -- the island of the oldest trees, out in the fog. She and Luna take the ferry across, with Mossy along as their guide ("I know the island. Slowly. But I know it."). On the far shore, before the forest, Luna has her first meeting with the sea: she gallops the beach, bites the surf, barks tremendous opinions at the waves, and is personally offended each time they retreat -- and then delighted each time they return.

3. **The adventure.** Into the rainforest. Everything hushes -- even Luna, which almost never happens. Moss curtains, green light, giant trees with bark like riverbeds. Digby the Mole pops up from a tunnel with underground news: the roots say the Eldest Tree has fallen into a sleep too deep, and the rain sleeps with her, because the Eldest is the one who calls it. Clover the Deer appears silently between the trees, the way she does, and leads them along the deer paths to the heart of the forest, where the Eldest Tree stands -- vast, warm, and profoundly asleep.

4. **The twist (part one -- the failed attempts, in threes).** They try to wake her. Luna delivers her mightiest roar -- the moss swallows it down to a polite "mmph." Digby rounds up the woodpeckers for a percussion section -- the Eldest sleeps on. Mossy suggests waiting ("It works for most things") -- they wait; nothing. Then Clover, who almost never speaks, says quietly: the Eldest has listened to every story on this island for a thousand years -- every bird's gossip, every root's rumor, every rainy-night tale. But nobody has ever once told a story *to her*. She hasn't fallen asleep like a tree. She's fallen asleep like someone waiting for a bedtime story that never came.

5. **The resolution.** Savannah climbs into the hollow between two great roots, right up close to the trunk, and tells the Eldest Tree a story -- a good-morning story, about a sun that rises, a kettle that sings, and a kingdom that wakes up green. As she speaks, the sap begins to stir; leaves shiver awake far overhead; and the Eldest wakes gently, with a laugh like creaking wood, and says the first thing she's said in a hundred years: "Ohh. I *liked* that." Then she stretches her branches, and calls the rain.

6. **Happily ever after.** The rain comes back as the softest silver mist, and the whole forest drinks -- moss plumping, ferns unrolling, everything gleaming. The friends spend the night dry and cozy in the root hollow while the rain whispers on a billion leaves above them (the best sleeping-sound there is). In the morning, the ferry home -- and a promise: Savannah will come back, every year, to tell the Eldest Tree one new story. Even the oldest, wisest ones need a story of their own at bedtime.

# Outline: The Village of the Friendly Longboats

**Premise:** Savannah and Luna sail with Big Al to a snug seaside village of big-bearded, big-hearted boat-folk preparing their midsummer feast -- but the village's beloved sunbread has lost its secret, and the old baker who knew it lives across the water in a log cabin deep in the woods, which turns out to be more magical than anyone remembered. The twist: the recipe was never lost, because the baker never had it whole -- long ago she gave each family in the village one piece of it, so sunbread could only ever be baked together. (Part of the Grand Journey set.)
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Luna, Big Al, Pepper the Skunk, Greta the Badger (new -- the old baker)
**Setting:** Solvik, a village of painted wooden houses tucked around a bright little bay, where every rooftop is trimmed like a ship and the harbor is full of longboats with carved dragon heads on their prows. Across the water and up a fern-lined path: Greta's log cabin, whose chimney smoke smells like cinnamon from a mile away.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Big Al -- retired boat-captain, still keeps his old longboat trim -- sails Savannah and Luna up the coast to Solvik for the midsummer feast: longest day of the year, long tables on the dock, lanterns strung between the mastheads. Comic beat on arrival: Luna meets the carved dragon heads on the longboat prows, greets them politely, gets no answer, and spends the rest of the visit keeping one suspicious eye on them. Pepper the Skunk has come along too -- she has the finest nose in the kingdom and has always wanted to smell a proper sea.

2. **The problem.** The village is in a quiet flap. The heart of every midsummer feast is sunbread -- golden, braided, warm-spiced, the bread every villager grew up on. But the old baker, Greta, retired across the water years ago, and this year, no one can make it come out right. The ovens work. The flour is good. The bread is fine. But it isn't sunbread. And the feast is in two days.

3. **The call.** Big Al rubs his beard: he knows Greta from the old days -- they sailed together before she took up baking. He knows the way to her cabin. Savannah, Luna, Pepper, and Big Al row across the bay at dawn and follow the fern path into the deep woods.

4. **The adventure.** The log cabin is magical in a comfortable, unbragging way: its chimney smoke smells of cinnamon a mile off, its door opens just before you knock, the kettle is already singing when you step inside, and there is always exactly one more chair than there are guests. Greta the Badger -- round, flour-dusted, sharp-eyed, warm -- welcomes them like family. But when they ask for the sunbread recipe, she shakes her head: she can't give it to them. Not won't. *Can't.*

5. **The twist.** Greta never had the recipe whole. Long ago, when she was young and the village was new, she split it on purpose: she gave each family in Solvik one piece -- the honey line to one house, the kneading song to another, the secret spice to a third, the count of the braid, the words you say when it goes in the oven. "No one bakes sunbread alone. That's not how the bread works. That's not how the village works." The village didn't lose the recipe. They forgot they each held a piece of it -- because as long as Greta was there to gather everyone in her bakery, nobody had to remember.

6. **The resolution.** Back across the water -- Greta comes too, first visit in years. The whole village gathers on the dock, and the recipe reassembles family by family, call-and-response: "We have the honey line!" "We have the kneading song!" Pepper's magnificent nose settles the great cardamom-or-cinnamon debate (it's both, and she names the third spice nobody could remember). Luna warms the great feast ovens with gentle dragon breath, the way Greta says the ovens were warmed in the old days. The braids go in. The whole dock smells like every midsummer anyone can remember. The sunbread rises golden.

7. **Happily ever after.** The midsummer feast: long tables end to end down the dock, lanterns strung between the mastheads, the longest golden evening of the year. Big Al and Greta trade thirty years of stories. Pepper sits at the head table as Keeper of the Spice. Each family writes its piece of the recipe into the village book -- one page per family, so it still takes everyone. Luna falls asleep under the table full of crumbs, and the carved dragon heads on the prows watch over the bay -- and if one of them winks at her, well, no one can prove it.

# Outline: The Letter That Wouldn't Hold Still

**Series:** The Prince Who Kept Showing Up, 6 of 10
**Premise:** Spencer borrows a bottle of Wilma's Honest Ink to write Savannah a letter, not knowing its one rule: only words that are simple and true stay on the page. His long, fancy, sideways-spelled sentences hop off the paper and scatter across the kingdom, landing on signs and doors and turning the village into a muddle. Savannah follows the trail of misplaced words, sets every sign right, and never learns whose letter it was. What stays on Spencer's page is three words, and they are not the three he expected.
**Characters:** Princess Savannah, Prince Spencer, Luna, Wilma, Pip the Jumping Spider, Thistle the Rabbit, Rascal the Raccoon
**Setting:** Spencer's little writing desk by a window. The village, with its painted signs: BAKERY, COBBLER, THE FRIENDLY GOOSE INN, and a hundred more. Wilma's shelf of very particular bottles.

## Beats

1. **The peaceful kingdom.** Prince Spencer had a secret, and the whole kingdom knew it but one. He has decided that if he cannot say it, he will write it. But his spelling has always come out sideways, and his sentences go on longer than they mean to, so he asks Wilma for ink that will help. Wilma hands him a small blue bottle labeled *Honest Ink* and says only, "Write plainly." He does not ask what she means.

2. **The problem.** He writes all night. Two pages, both sides, every big word he knows, spelled his own way. At dawn the words begin to fidget. Then one hops off the page. Then a dozen. Then the whole letter lifts off like a flock of sparrows and pours out the open window. By breakfast the BAKERY sign says BEAUTIFUL, the COBBLER's door says COURAGEOUS, and the Friendly Goose Inn is now, according to its sign, THE MAGNIFISENT GOOSE. Nobody can find anything.

3. **The call.** Savannah walks into a village where every sign says something lovely and none of them say what they are. She thinks it is the best puzzle she has seen in ages. She recruits Pip, who notices details nobody else does, and Thistle, who can outrun a word, and Rascal, who can climb to any sign. Spencer arrives, pale, and offers to help "for no reason." Luna sniffs him and knows exactly what happened.

4. **The adventure.** They hunt words all over the kingdom. Pip spots the tiny ones hiding in the cracks of the well. Thistle chases a fast one ("ALWAYS") three times around the mill. Rascal climbs for "FOREVER," which has settled on the church weathervane. Savannah sorts them as they come in, and she notices they are all kind words, warm words, and all spelled a little wrong. "Somebody wrote a very nice letter," she says. "And couldn't spell." Spencer coughs.

5. **The twist.** With every sign set right, Savannah hands the pile of runaway words back to Wilma, who explains the ink: it keeps only what is simple and true, and lets the rest go wander. Spencer runs home to his desk. Of two whole pages, three words remain on the paper, in his own handwriting, spelled correctly for once in his life: *Just say it.* The ink has written him a letter back.

6. **The resolution.** Savannah never asks whose letter it was. The village signs are back to BAKERY and COBBLER, though the innkeeper keeps MAGNIFICENT (correctly spelled by Savannah) because business has never been better. Wilma takes her bottle back and says Spencer can borrow it again "when you're ready to write three words." Spencer folds the page with *Just say it* and keeps it in his pocket. It stays there for the rest of the series.

7. **Happily ever after.** That evening Savannah finds one last word she missed, tucked in Luna's collar: "YOU." She keeps it, because it is a good word and clearly belongs to someone. She puts it on her windowsill, where the sun can find it.
